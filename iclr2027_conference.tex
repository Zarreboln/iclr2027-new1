% Visual Place Recognition (VPR) identifies where a photograph was taken by matching it against a geo-tagged database. 
% % Two families dominate. 
% Supervised methods learn an aggregation layer from place labels and are strong on the street-level imagery they are trained for, but they call for corpora running to tens of millions of labelled images and lose a large part of their accuracy once the imagery changes. Training-free methods drop that cost and generalise better, yet they decide what to keep from an image by salience, a property that does not separate one place from another, and the features they obtain leave them behind on accuracy. 
% % To address both, 
% We propose \method{}, a training-free pipeline built entirely on patch-to-patch similarity. Within one image, self-similarity gives every patch a distinctiveness, the inverse of its multiplicity within the image, so a scene element counts once however much of the frame it covers. Between two images, cross-similarity is read twice, once to scan a database and once to fine-rank a shortlist.
% % \textcolor{blue}{no place labels for fitting its representation}
% Under one fixed configuration \method{} reaches the highest mean Recall@1 on seventeen benchmarks, $93.2$ on eight standard VPR benchmarks and $76.6$ on nine cross-environment benchmarks against $65.1$ for the strongest supervised method, while edging that method on the standard benchmarks its supervision targets. As a plug-in scoring rule it improves two existing two-stage pipelines by $+5.6$ and $+9.7$ R@1 and eight single-stage ones by $+9.1$ to $+17.8$.

% \textcolor{red}{Visual Place Recognition (VPR) localises an image by retrieving the geo-referenced database image that shows the same place. Most VPR methods are training-based, aiming to learn effective features from annotated data. These methods achieve strong results on standard VPR benchmarks but require millions of labelled data. Even with such extensive training, they still fail on other VPR benchmarks that differ from their training set. Training-free VPR methods avoid this annotation cost and generalise better. However, they decide what to keep from an image by salience, a property that does not separate one place from another, so the resulting features are not discriminative enough to achieve good accuracy on standard VPR benchmarks. In this paper, we propose PaSS \textcolor{green}{[need to explain what PaSS stands for here]}, a training-free pipeline built solely on patch-to-patch similarity. Given a query or database image, only a frozen backbone is applied to extract patch features. We then perform a series of patch-to-patch similarity-based comparisons for VPR: self-similarity for patch importance, followed by a weighted second-moment patch feature pooling for coarse ranking, and cross-image patch-to-patch similarity for fine ranking. Based on comprehensive evaluation on ~\textbf{17} benchmarks, PaSS achieves state-of-the-art results on both the 8 standard VPR benchmarks (\textbf{+13\%} over the best training-free baseline and \textbf{+0.3\%} over the best trained baseline) and the 9 cross-environment benchmarks (\textbf{+7.1\%} over the best training-free baseline and \textbf{+11.5\%} over the best trained baseline, despite the latter's heavy annotation requirements). In addition, the proposed module can be easily plugged into existing methods, improving their performance by \textbf{+5.6\%} to up to \textbf{+17.8\%}.}

\textcolor{red}{Visual Place Recognition (VPR) localises an image by retrieving the geo-referenced database image that shows the same place. Most VPR methods are training-based, aiming to learn effective features from annotated data. These methods achieve strong results on standard VPR benchmarks but require millions of labelled samples, i.e., the annotation cost challenge. Even with such extensive training, they still fail on other VPR benchmarks that differ from their training set, such as cross-environment benchmarks. Training-free VPR methods avoid this annotation cost and generalise better. However, these methods do not fully exploit pretrained vision models, and the resulting features are not discriminative enough to achieve good accuracy on standard VPR benchmarks, i.e., the vision model utilisation challenge. To tackle these challenges, we propose PaSS \textcolor{green}{[need to explain what PaSS stands for here]}, a training-free pipeline built solely on patch similarity. Given a query or database image, only a frozen backbone is applied to extract patch features. We then perform a series of patch similarity-based comparisons for VPR: self-similarity for patch importance, followed by weighted second-moment patch feature pooling for coarse ranking, and cross-image patch similarity for re-ranking. Based on comprehensive evaluation on \textbf{17} benchmarks, PaSS achieves state-of-the-art results on both the 8 standard VPR benchmarks (\textbf{+13\%} over the best training-free baseline and \textbf{+0.3\%} over the best trained baseline) and the 9 cross-environment benchmarks (\textbf{+7.1\%} over the best training-free baseline and \textbf{+11.5\%} over the best trained baseline, despite the latter's heavy annotation requirements). In addition, the proposed module can be easily plugged into existing methods, improving their performance by \textbf{+5.6\%} to up to \textbf{+17.8\%}.}

\end{abstract}
