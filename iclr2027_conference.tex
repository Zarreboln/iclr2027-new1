\begin{abstract}
Visual Place Recognition (VPR) identifies where a photograph was taken by matching it against a geo-tagged database. 
Two families dominate. Supervised methods learn an aggregation layer from place labels and are strong on the urban benchmarks they are trained for, but they call for corpora running to tens of millions of labelled images and lose a large part of their accuracy once the imagery changes. Training-free methods drop that cost and generalise better, yet they decide what to keep from an image by salience, a property that does not separate one place from another, and the features they obtain leave them behind on accuracy. To address both, we propose \method{}, a training-free pipeline built entirely on patch-to-patch similarity. Within an image, self-similarity gives every patch an importance in closed form, so a scene element counts once however much of the frame it covers; between two images, cross-similarity is read twice, once to scan a database and once to re-rank a shortlist. Nothing is fitted between the frozen backbone and the ranking.
% \textcolor{blue}{no place labels for fitting its representation}
Under one fixed configuration \method{} reaches the highest mean Recall@1 on thirteen benchmarks, $93.3$ on the urban suite and $76.6$ across nine further environments against $65.1$ for the strongest supervised method, while matching that method on the urban benchmarks its supervision targets. As a plug-in scoring rule it improves four published two-stage pipelines by $+5.5$ to $+12.5$ R@1 and eight single-stage ones by $+9.1$ to $+17.8$.
\end{abstract}
