\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:exp}
We evaluate the pipeline from two perspectives. First, we compare it with training-free and with trained methods on seventeen benchmarks, eight standard VPR benchmarks and nine cross-environment benchmarks (\S\ref{sec:sota}). Second, we ask which part of the pipeline the result comes from (\S\ref{sec:swap}). Every number for our method comes from one configuration; nothing is tuned per benchmark.

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\noindent\begin{minipage}[t]{0.585\textwidth}
\vspace{0pt}
\paragraph{Datasets.} We evaluate on seventeen benchmarks, in two groups in Table~\ref{tab:datasets}: eight standard VPR benchmarks, the seven street-level benchmarks that form the domain of the corpora VPR methods are trained on (GSV-Cities, SF-XL, MSLS) plus the Nordland railway, and nine cross-environment benchmarks, the suite of \citet{anyloc}. Supplementary~\S\ref{app:protocol} gives splits, ground-truth tolerances and the conventions on which published numbers differ.

\paragraph{Implementation details.} The feature extractor is a frozen DINOv2 or DINOv3 backbone; we set $\tau{=}0.5$ and $\theta{=}0.65$ for all benchmarks. Baselines use their own default settings; Supplementary~\S\ref{app:impl} lists the remaining details.

\paragraph{Evaluation metrics.} We report Recall@1 (R@1) under the standard protocol \citep{netvlad,patchnetvlad}: a query counts as localized if the top-ranked database image lies within the tolerance of its benchmark, following the order of Table~\ref{tab:datasets}. For the standard benchmarks this is $25$\,m, except Nordland ($\pm10$ frames), Oxford RobotCar ($16$\,m) and AmsterTime (the paired present-day image); for the cross-environment benchmarks it is the provided correspondences or pose thresholds (Supplementary~\S\ref{app:protocol}).

\subsection{Comparison with state-of-the-art}
\label{sec:sota}
\paragraph{Against training-free methods.} Table~\ref{tab:breadth} compares the pipeline with every training-free method we are aware of on all seventeen benchmarks. On the standard benchmarks we lead the best prior training-free number on seven of the eight benchmarks and are within $0.5$ of AnyLoc on Oxford, for a mean of $93.3$ against $80.3$ for EffoVPR-ZS; the largest margin is Nordland, $+38.7$, whose seasonal change no training-free method handled before. On the cross-environment benchmarks we are best or tied on all nine, $76.6$ against $69.5$ for AnyLoc, whose descriptor is $24\times$ larger than ours. The frozen class token and GeM, which only pool, reach $51.5$ and $56.7$ on the standard benchmarks; the rest of the way to $93.3$ comes from how the tokens are read.

\paragraph{Against trained methods.} On the standard benchmarks, the domain their supervision targets (Table~\ref{tab:trained}), our mean of $93.3$ edges the strongest of them, SAGE at $93.0$, with the best number on Tokyo 24/7, Nordland and AmsterTime. On the cross-environment benchmarks the thirteen trained methods above $80$ on the standard ones each give up $27$ to $40$ mean R@1, ours gives up $16.7$, and we take the best number on seven of the nine benchmarks, $76.6$ against $65.1$ for MegaLoc. The margins are largest where a single descriptor has least to work with: $67.0$ against $38.1$ on Hawkins, $83.0$ against $60.7$ in Laurel Caverns and $77.6$ against $45.6$ on VP-Air. The price is latency: a query costs $47$\,ms on Pitts30k, $31.5$ of them the DINOv2 forward pass, against $35$ to $67$\,ms for the two-stage baselines (Supplementary~\S\ref{app:cost}).

\subsection{Ablation study}
\label{sec:swap}
\paragraph{Our second stage in other pipelines.} Table~\ref{tab:stage2swap} drops our re-ranking rule into other pipelines: each method keeps its resolution and shortlist, and only the scoring rule changes. Replacing the re-ranker of SelaVPR, R2Former and Pair-VPR raises their mean over the nine cross-environment benchmarks by $+9.7$, $+5.6$ and $+1.9$ R@1, better on $18$ of $27$ cells; adding it to eight single-stage methods improves all of them, by $+9.1$ (MegaLoc) to $+17.8$ (SuperVLAD), better on $63$ of $72$ cells. The gain is largest where a single descriptor is weakest, and the rule needs no training and no access to a method's internals.

\paragraph{Is it the backbone?} To compare aggregation at equal capacity we retrain three baselines on DINOv3 under their published recipes (Table~\ref{tab:perdataset}). Our mean is $82.1$ against $73.1$ for the strongest retrained baseline; we are within one point of the best on eleven of the thirteen benchmarks and ahead by more than $14$ on three. A method that learns its aggregation pays for every new backbone in labelled data and fine-tuning; a training-free pipeline adopts it by loading it, at no cost in accuracy.
