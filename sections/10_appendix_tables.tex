\subsection{Implementation details}
\label{app:impl}
All experiments share one configuration. The backbone is a frozen DINOv3 for our best results and DINOv2 for the \emph{v2} rows; its value-facet tokens at $672$px (DINOv2) or $768$px (DINOv3) are reduced to $C{=}512$ channels by PCA. The coarse descriptor $S_C$ is reduced to $1{,}024$ dimensions by whitened PCA. The ablations were run before the descriptor was halved and are reported at $2{,}048$: the calibration of $\tau$ and $\theta$ (\S\ref{app:calib}), the component ablation (\S\ref{app:components}), the older backbones (\S\ref{app:oldbackbones}), the patch importance signals (Table~\ref{tab:w-signal} and \S\ref{app:recallk}), the coarse ranking of \S\ref{app:coarsegrid} and the timings of \S\ref{app:cost}. Halving the descriptor costs $0.1$ mean R@1 on the standard group and nothing on the cross-environment group, so none of those comparisons turns on it. We set $\tau{=}0.5$ and $\theta{=}0.65$ for all benchmarks (Supplementary~\S\ref{app:calib}) and a shortlist of $K{=}\min(1000,|\mathcal{D}|)$.

\subsection{Calibration of $\tau$ and $\theta$}
\label{app:hyper}
\label{app:calib}
Both thresholds are calibrated once on SF-XL val, a split that appears in no result table, and fixed for every benchmark.

The gate of Eq.~(\ref{eq:stage2}) keeps a query patch when its best match $m_i$ exceeds $\theta$: the likelihood that a match is genuine rather than accidental increases with $m_i$, so a fixed threshold is the Bayes-optimal rule.

\section{Analysis}
\subsection{Derivation of the patch weights}
\label{app:ridge}
Stack the $\ell_2$-normalized tokens of one image row-wise as $F$ and write $S(F,F)=FF^{\top}$, the within-image table of \S\ref{sec:prelim}. The condition of \citet{democratic} asks that every token contribute equally to its image, which \citet{gmp} write as $S(F,F)\,\mathbf w=\mathbf 1$. With $N\gg C$ the matrix $S(F,F)=FF^{\top}$ has rank at most $C$, so $\mathbf 1$ lies outside its range and the system has no exact solution; \citet{gmp} add a ridge term and solve iteratively. We impose the condition on the thresholded matrix $\hat S=\ind[\,S(F,F)>\tau\,]$ instead.

Under the burst model an element seen $n$ times yields $n$ patches near-duplicate of one another and of nothing else, so $\hat S$ is block diagonal with one all-ones block per element: $\hat S=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{m}J_{n_k}$ with $J_n$ the $n\times n$ all-ones matrix. The system decouples, and block $k$ reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
  J_{n_k}w^{(k)}=\mathbf 1_{n_k}
  \iff
  \Big(\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathrm{block}(k)}w_i\Big)\mathbf 1_{n_k}=\mathbf 1_{n_k},
\end{equation}
one scalar equation in $n_k$ unknowns. Two independent arguments pick the same point out of that affine set. The patches of a block are exchangeable, so a solution respecting the symmetry is constant on the block, and Eq.~(\ref{eq:block}) forces the constant to be $1/n_k$. And the minimum-norm solution gives the same,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pinv}
  w=\hat S^{\dagger}\mathbf 1,
  \qquad
  J_n^{\dagger}\mathbf 1_n=\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}J_n\mathbf 1_n=\tfrac1n\mathbf 1_n .
\end{equation}
It is also where \citet{gmp}'s ridge term goes in the limit: $(J_n+\lambda I)^{-1}\mathbf 1_n=\tfrac{1}{n+\lambda}\mathbf 1_n\to\tfrac1n\mathbf 1_n$ as $\lambda\to0^{+}$. The block sizes need no separate recovery, since $n_k$ is the row sum of $\hat S$ at any $i$ in block $k$, leaving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:w}
  \hat S=\ind\big[\,S(F,F)>\tau\,\big]
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  w_i=\frac{1}{n_i}\quad\text{with}\quad n_i=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat S_{ij} .
\end{equation}
Only the threshold does not follow from the model: one number, $\tau{=}0.5$, fixed for every backbone and dataset. Binarizing is the modelling step, and it is also what fixes the exponent at one: frozen ViT tokens occupy a narrow cone \citep{modalitygap,anisotropy}, so the raw row sums are two to three times less dispersed within an image than the thresholded counts, and a distinctiveness built on them needs a larger exponent to match.

\paragraph{Beyond the block model.} The block model asks that thresholded similarity be transitive: if patch $i$ resembles $j$ and $j$ resembles $k$, then $i$ resembles $k$. Real images break this in three ways. Gradual content, sky shading from bright to dark or a wall receding in perspective, forms a chain in which neighbours clear $\tau$ but the two ends do not, so $\hat S$ is banded rather than block-diagonal and patches in the middle of the chain receive a smaller $w$ than the rule intends. A patch on the boundary of two elements, a window frame against its wall, clears $\tau$ with both, so its $n_i$ counts both blocks and the row sums of its neighbours no longer reach one. And a few pairs inside one element fall just below $\tau$ under a change of lighting or a partial occlusion, splitting the element into overlapping sub-blocks with slightly different $n_i$. In all three cases $w_i=1/n_i$ still decreases with the amount of similar content around patch $i$, which is the property both stages rely on; what is lost is only the exact equality in Eq.~(\ref{eq:demo}). Table~\ref{tab:residual} measures the residual $r_i=|(\hat S\mathbf w)_i-1|$ on real images: its mean is $0.22$ to $0.27$ across 8 benchmarks, half of all tokens satisfy the condition to within $0.2$ and about nine in ten to within $0.5$, with the aerial and underwater sets, whose imagery is the most uniform, furthest from the model. The threshold trades the two failure modes against each other, a higher $\tau$ splitting elements and a lower one merging them through chains and boundary patches, which is what the sweep in Table~\ref{tab:cal-tau} reflects.

\subsection{Why the second moment factorizes}
\label{app:factorize}
The coarse descriptor of \S\ref{sec:global} compares two images through $\langle S_C(Q),S_C(D)\rangle=\sum_{i,j} w_{Q,i}\,w_{D,j}\,S(Q,D)_{ij}^{2}$, where $w_{D,j}$ is the weight of database patch $j$: a contraction of the cross-similarity table from both sides, $\sum_{i,j} w_{Q,i}w_{D,j}\,\sigma(S_{ij})$ with $S=S(Q,D)$ and $\sigma(u)=u^{2}$ applied entrywise. The same regrouping holds for any monomial $\sigma(u)=u^{\gamma}$: the entrywise power $S^{\circ\gamma}$ equals $Q^{[\gamma]}\big(D^{[\gamma]}\big)^{\top}$, where $Q^{[\gamma]}$ is the row-wise Kronecker power whose $i$-th row is $\mathbf q_i^{\otimes\gamma}$. The contraction therefore becomes one inner product of width $C^{\gamma}$ between two vectors, each computed from a single image. At $\gamma{=}1$ that vector is the weighted mean of the tokens, the first-order instance of a VLAD-style descriptor; at $\gamma{=}2$ it is the weighted second moment $S_C$ we deploy.
