\subsection{Implementation details}
\label{app:impl}
One configuration is used for every benchmark. Tokens are the value facet of a frozen backbone, DINOv2 ViT-G/14 at block $31$ for \emph{v2} and DINOv3 ViT-7B/16 at block $32$ for \emph{v3}, taken at $672$px and $768$px respectively so that both yield $2{,}304$ patches, and reduced to $C{=}512$ channels by a token-PCA. Patch importance is $w_i{=}1/n_i$ at $\tau{=}0.5$, computed on the full token set. Stage 1 stores the second moment $F^{\top}WF$ built from each image's $1024$ most important tokens, $\ell_2$-normalized and reduced to $2048$ dimensions by a whitened descriptor-PCA, and scans to a shortlist of $K{=}\min(1000,|\mathcal{D}|)$ over the deployment database $\mathcal{D}$. Stage 2 re-ranks by \eqref{eq:stage2} at $\theta{=}0.65$, scoring those $1024$ tokens on the urban benchmarks and all $2{,}304$ on the environments (Appendix~\ref{app:s2grid}). All runs are on one H100; layer, facet, shortlist depth and every constant are swept in Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}.

\paragraph{Why a threshold is the right form for the gate.} Write the row maximum $m_i$ of \eqref{eq:stage2} as evidence for the hypothesis that patch $i$ has a counterpart in the candidate, against the alternative that its best match is accidental. The likelihood ratio between the two increases with $m_i$, so the Bayes-optimal decision is to accept patch $i$ when $m_i$ exceeds a constant \citep{kay}. The gate of \eqref{eq:stage2} is that decision, with the constant fixed at $\theta{=}0.65$ for every backbone and dataset; the sweep below reports what other values cost.

\subsection{Derivation of the closed form}
\label{app:ridge}

Stack the $\ell_2$-normalized tokens of one image row-wise as $F$ and write $G=FF^{\top}$, the within-image table $S(F,F)$ of \S\ref{sec:prelim}. The condition of \citet{democratic} asks that every token contribute equally to its image, which \citet{gmp} write as $Gw=\mathbf 1$. With $N\gg C$ the Gram matrix has rank at most $C$, so $\mathbf 1$ lies outside its range and the system has no exact solution; \citet{gmp} add a ridge term and solve iteratively. We impose the condition on the thresholded matrix $\hat G=\mathbb 1[\,G>\tau\,]$ instead.

Under the burst model an element seen $n$ times yields $n$ patches near-duplicate of one another and of nothing else, so $\hat G$ is block diagonal with one all-ones block per element: $\hat G=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{m}J_{n_k}$ with $J_n$ the $n\times n$ all-ones matrix. The system decouples, and block $k$ reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block}
  J_{n_k}w^{(k)}=\mathbf 1_{n_k}
  \iff
  \Big(\textstyle\sum_{i\in\mathrm{block}(k)}w_i\Big)\mathbf 1_{n_k}=\mathbf 1_{n_k},
\end{equation}
one scalar equation in $n_k$ unknowns. Two independent arguments pick the same point out of that affine set. The patches of a block are exchangeable, so a solution respecting the symmetry is constant on the block, and \eqref{eq:block} forces the constant to be $1/n_k$. And the minimum-norm solution gives the same,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pinv}
  w=\hat G^{\dagger}\mathbf 1,
  \qquad
  J_n^{\dagger}\mathbf 1_n=\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}J_n\mathbf 1_n=\tfrac1n\mathbf 1_n .
\end{equation}
It is also where \citet{gmp}'s ridge term goes in the limit: $(J_n+\lambda I)^{-1}\mathbf 1_n=\tfrac{1}{n+\lambda}\mathbf 1_n\to\tfrac1n\mathbf 1_n$ as $\lambda\to0^{+}$. The block sizes need no separate recovery, since $n_k$ is the row sum of $\hat G$ at any $i$ in block $k$, leaving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:w}
  \hat G=\mathbb 1\big[\,G>\tau\,\big]
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  w_i=\frac{1}{n_i}\quad\text{with}\quad n_i=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\hat G_{ij} .
\end{equation}
Only the threshold does not follow from the model: one number, $\tau{=}0.5$, fixed for every backbone and dataset. Binarizing is the modelling step, and it is also what fixes the exponent at one: frozen ViT tokens occupy a narrow cone \citep{modalitygap,anisotropy}, so the raw row sums are two to three times less dispersed within an image than the thresholded counts, and an importance built on them needs a larger exponent to match.

\section{First stage: the second-moment descriptor}

\subsection{Why the second moment factorizes}
\label{app:factorize}
Section~\ref{sec:global} contracts the cross-similarity table from both sides, $\sum_{i,j} w_{Q,i}w_{D,j}\,\sigma(S_{ij})$ with $\sigma$ applied entrywise, and states the case $\sigma(u)=u^{2}$. The same regrouping holds for any monomial $\sigma(u)=u^{\gamma}$: the entrywise power $S^{\circ\gamma}$ equals $Q^{[\gamma]}\big(D^{[\gamma]}\big)^{\top}$, where $Q^{[\gamma]}$ is the row-wise Kronecker power whose $i$-th row is $\mathbf q_i^{\otimes\gamma}$. The contraction therefore becomes one inner product of width $C^{\gamma}$ between two vectors, each computed from a single image. At $\gamma{=}1$ that vector is the weighted mean of the tokens, the first-order instance of a VLAD-style descriptor; at $\gamma{=}2$ it is the weighted second moment $G_C$ we deploy. the factorial in \S\ref{sec:compact} measures the devices that act on it.

\section{Second stage: \textsc{MaxSim} re-ranking}

\subsection{Why \textsc{MaxSim} admits no fixed-size descriptor}
\label{app:nofactor}
The first stage factorizes because its contraction is bilinear in the two token sets. The row-wise maximum of \eqref{eq:stage2} is not: it is not linear in the candidate's tokens, so no regrouping of the kind used in Appendix~\ref{app:factorize} applies. The score is not even symmetric, which rules out any form $\langle\psi(Q),\psi(D)\rangle$ with $\psi$ mapping a token set to a vector. Take $Q=\{\mathbf q\}$ and $D=\{\mathbf d_1,\mathbf d_2\}$ with unit weights and $S=(0.9,\ 0.1)$: scoring $Q$ against $D$ gives $0.9$, while scoring $D$ against $Q$ gives $0.9+0.1=1.0$. A pipeline that stores one vector per image therefore cannot express this stage, and we run it pair by pair on the shortlist.
