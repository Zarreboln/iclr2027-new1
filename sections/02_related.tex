\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

{\color{orange}{\subsection{Supervised and training-free VPR.}}
Supervised VPR learns image representations from place labels. NetVLAD \citep{netvlad} integrates feature aggregation into a trainable retrieval model. CosPlace and EigenPlaces~\citep{cosplace,eigenplaces} formulate training as classification over geographic cells. MixVPR~\citep{mixvpr} aggregates features through feed-forward mixing, and CliqueMining~\citep{cliquemining} samples positive and negative examples from cliques of visually similar images. 
SALAD~\citep{salad} and BoQ~\citep{boq} develop both aggregation and adaptation strategies based on strong pretrained backbones such as DINOv2~\citep{dinov2}. SuperVLAD~\citep{supervlad} removes cluster-centre subtraction while retaining learned assignments, and EMVP~\citep{emvp} uses second-order features for centroid-free probing. CricaVPR~\citep{cricavpr} models cross-image correlations, while SelaVPR and SelaVPR++~\citep{selavpr,selavprpp} learn lightweight adaptations of frozen backbones.

Pretrained visual features support image comparison without additional task-specific training. \citet{vitfacets} show that features from a self-supervised vision transformer can serve as dense descriptors for visual correspondence. AnyLoc \citep{anyloc} combines frozen features with unsupervised VLAD aggregation. SegVLAD \citep{segvlad} uses segment-based retrieval to accommodate partial scene overlap and supports both generic and task-specialized encoders. Both rely on a criterion defined outside the image pair, a codebook or a segmenter. Our approach uses the repetition of patches within each image to determine their contribution, applying the same weights to aggregation and candidate matching.}

{\color{orange}\subsection{Feature aggregation, weighting and matching} 
Most VPR methods represent an image by a global descriptor aggregated from its local features and retrieve by nearest-neighbour search. Fisher vectors~\citep{fisherkernel,improvedfisher} and VLAD~\citep{allvlad} encode each descriptor relative to a learned codebook and sum the encodings per codeword, while second-order pooling~\citep{bcnn,mpncov} averages the outer products of the descriptors into a bilinear or covariance matrix. \citet{emk} show that the inner product between two pooled vectors approximates a sum of kernel values over all descriptor pairs, so weights on descriptors act as weights on descriptor pairs. Our first stage takes the codebook-free route and pools a weighted second moment per image. 

Discriminative patch mining~\citep{whatmakesparis,modeseeking} studies which local patterns distinguish image collections. 
Existed VPR works already weights local features by how well they identify a place, discarding features that recur across unrelated places~\citep{knopp}, down-weighting repeated structures~\citep{repetitive}, scoring descriptor distinctiveness~\citep{dislocation}, or learning location-specific classifiers~\citep{perlocation}. 
Democratic aggregation~\citep{democratic} uses iterative scaling to equalize weighted descriptors' contributions to the aggregate's self-similarity, and generalized max pooling~\citep{gmp} instead seeks equal descriptor-to-aggregate similarities through regularised least squares. SAGE~\citep{sage} learns residual patch weights before bilinear aggregation. Other weighting signals include visual-word inverse document frequency~\citep{videogoogle}, class-token attention~\citep{effovpr}, and pair-dependent cross-reference weights~\citep{deepemd}. Our method assigns unit total weight to each group, which connects to contribution balancing without iterative weight optimization. 

Two-stage VPR re-ranks the top-$k$ candidates by comparing the local features of the query with those of each candidate directly. Patch-NetVLAD~\citep{patchnetvlad} matches multi-scale patch descriptors with spatial verification, and SelaVPR~\citep{selavpr} counts mutual nearest neighbours between adapted local features. R2Former~\citep{r2former} learns re-ranking from local-feature correlations and spatial information, and Pair-VPR~\citep{pairvpr} trains a transformer to classify whether an image pair depicts the same place. FoL~\citep{fol} restricts matching to learned discriminative regions. EffoVPR~\citep{effovpr} use class-token-related attention scores to select local features and ranks candidates by the number of mutual nearest-neighbour matches above a similarity threshold, while our method scores a candidate with a confidence-gated \textsc{MaxSim} under the same weights as the first stage.

% \paragraph{What identifies a place.}
% The question of which parts of an image make a place recognizable predates retrieval benchmarks. \citet{whatmakesparis} mine street-level photographs for the visual elements that set one city apart from another, and \citet{modeseeking} formalize the search as discriminative mode seeking, looking for elements that are at once frequent within a collection and discriminative between collections. Inside VPR the same intuition appears as the suppression of content that cannot separate places: discarding features that recur across unrelated locations \citep{knopp}, grouping repeated facade structure and discounting each group once \citep{repetitive}, weighting descriptors by an estimate of their distinctiveness \citep{dislocation}, and learning a classifier per location \citep{perlocation}. Recent work keeps the same shape but learns the criterion, whether a reliability score over local regions \citep{fol}, class-token attention over patches \citep{effovpr}, or a reweighting of local features learned alongside a partially tuned backbone \citep{sage}; the largest systems instead widen the corpus until the criterion is learned from more places than any single dataset holds \citep{megaloc}. All of these settle what counts before the pair of images meets, either from a labelled corpus or from a criterion estimated elsewhere; we instead read it from the pair itself, and the estimate costs a threshold and a row sum.
